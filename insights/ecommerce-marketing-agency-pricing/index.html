Ecommerce Marketing Agency Pricing:
What It Actually Costs

Retainer, project, and performance-based pricing models explained plainly — so you can tell whether a quote is reasonable before you sign anything.

Ajay Harchandani
Founder & CEO, GrowthYug • Published September 27, 2026 • 8 min read

Almost every ecommerce marketing agency site lists services. Almost none list pricing. That's not always bad faith — scope genuinely varies — but it leaves founders guessing whether a quote is fair. Here's how pricing actually breaks down, and what drives the range.

1. The Three Pricing Models

Almost every agency quote is a variant of one of these:

  • Monthly retainer: A fixed fee for an agreed scope of work (e.g. Meta Ads management, or full-funnel growth). The most common model for ongoing work.
  • Project / fixed fee: A one-time quote for defined, finite work — a Shopify rebuild, a technical SEO audit, a brand launch package.
  • Performance / percentage-based: A percentage of managed ad spend (commonly 10-20%), or more rarely a revenue share. Aligns incentives with scale, but not automatically with profitability — see the red flags below.

2. What Actually Drives the Price

Two agencies quoting wildly different numbers for "Meta Ads management" are often quoting different things entirely:

  • Number of channels: a single-channel retainer (just Meta, or just Amazon) costs less than full-funnel management across paid, marketplace, SEO and CRO.
  • Ad spend under management: agencies managing ₹50L/month in spend need more senior attention than ones managing ₹3L/month, even at the same percentage fee.
  • Seniority of the team: a founder-led or senior-strategist engagement costs more than an account-manager-plus-junior-executor model, for good reason.
  • Reporting and analytics depth: custom dashboards, GA4 configuration and cohort-level reporting take real hours that a template report doesn't.

4. Pricing Red Flags to Watch For

  • No minimum retainer with pure percentage-of-spend pricing: creates an incentive to grow spend even when profitability should say otherwise.
  • Vague "success fee" language with no defined metric: ask exactly what triggers the fee and how it's calculated.
  • Long lock-in with no scope review clause: scope and spend both change — pricing should have a built-in review point.
  • A quote with no scope breakdown at all: if you can't tell what's actually included, you can't compare it to anything.

6. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an ecommerce marketing agency cost?

Retainer-based agencies in India typically range from roughly ₹40,000 to ₹3,00,000+ per month depending on scope, brand size, and whether ad spend disbursal is included. Project fees are usually quoted flat; performance-based deals shift some risk to the agency.

Is percentage-of-ad-spend pricing a good model?

It aligns agency incentives with scaling budget, but can reward spending more rather than spending better. It works best paired with a minimum retainer floor and profitability targets, not spend growth alone.
